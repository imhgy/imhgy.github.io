<h1 id="Hello" style="margin-top: 1rem">👋 Hi!</h1>

I am a [**HIIT Postdoctoral Fellow**](https://www.hiit.fi/current-fellows) at **Aalto University** and am also affiliated with [**ELLIS Institute Finland**](https://www.ellisinstitute.fi/), working with Prof. [Guoying Zhao](https://gyzhao-nm.github.io/Guoying/) and Prof. [Juho Kannala](https://users.aalto.fi/~kannalj1/). I received my **Ph.D. from Xi’an Jiaotong University**, supervised by Prof. [Xinyu Yang](https://gr.xjtu.edu.cn/xyyang/en/index.htm). Previously, I spent three years at **Queen Mary University of London**, where I worked as a **Research Associate** with Prof. [Dimitrios Kollias](https://sites.google.com/view/dimitrioskollias).

My research examines **the mechanisms and limits of AI’s understanding of human affect and behaviour**. I investigate how psychological theories can inform learned representations, how those representations shape model judgements, and why the resulting errors and biases vary across people and contexts. This analysis guides my development of **interpretable and fair learning methods**, linking **psychological theory with mathematical optimisation**. My long-term goal is to advance **human-centred and trustworthy AI** that can reliably understand human behaviour across diverse social contexts.

<div class="published-database">
  <div class="section-header">
    <h2>Towards Fair and Consistent Affect Analysis</h2>
    <a class="code-link" href="https://github.com/dkollias/Fair-Consistent-Affect-Analysis/tree/main" target="_blank">Access</a>
  </div>

  <p>
    Evaluating affective computing methods remains challenging due to inconsistencies in database partitioning and evaluation protocols, which often result in unfair or biased performance comparisons. While many studies report steady performance gains, our analysis questions the validity of such claims. Motivated by these insights, we introduce a unified evaluation protocol to ensure fairness and comparability across benchmarks.
  </p>

  <p>
    Specifically, for six widely used affective datasets, we provide comprehensive demographic annotations—including <strong>race</strong>, <strong>gender</strong>, and <strong>age</strong>—alongside standardized evaluation metrics and a shared protocol for <strong>expression recognition</strong>, <strong>action unit detection</strong>, and <strong>valence-arousal estimation</strong>.
  </p>

  <p>
    Additionally, we release multiple pre-trained models (ranging from baselines to state-of-the-art systems) trained under this unified protocol. A dedicated leaderboard is also introduced to encourage future research with a stronger emphasis on fairness, consistency, and reproducibility.
  </p>
</div>
